reasoning: This is the direct by-product of refusing to stop at the prepaid refutation. I generalised the sweep from "does any write body carry a subscriptionId" to "does any write body carry ANY identity handle", expecting a confirmation of the clean result and got the opposite: 39 distinct write routes repo-wide take a body-supplied user identifier, systematically, across all three versions and four product families. `userId` is `required: true` on most of them. The shapes that matter are the ones where the body names WHO and the URL names WHAT: `POST /v3/signls/{signlId}/acknowledge` takes `{signlId}` in the path and `userId` in the body, meaning the request identifies both the alarm being acknowledged and the person credited with acknowledging it. Duty routes are the same: `POST /v3/duties/punchIn` takes a required `userId` and writes an attendance record. For a SIGL/alerting platform the actor of an acknowledgement is a record of record and a compliance artifact, so if it comes from the payload rather than the token, every one of these routes is an attribution-forgery surface reachable with one ordinary integration credential. This is also the same shape as the parked `userLicenses` IDOR one layer out — that item asks whether a path-segment `{id}` is validated against the caller, this asks whether a body-supplied `userId` is — and if the answer is no anywhere, it is no systematically rather than once.
